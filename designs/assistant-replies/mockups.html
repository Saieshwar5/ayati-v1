One switch decides how your assistant handles a chat

Today you start a "thread" in two different ways, give an instruction each time, and manage it with Stop, Resume and Delete. The proposal replaces all of that with one switch in the chat's header that has three positions.

The chat below is a working example with canned replies. No model runs and nothing in the app has changed.

How Ayati's messages look in the chat

A message Ayati wrote carries its name as a mention, @Ayati, on a line above the message. Under the message there is only the time, as on every other message. An assistant with another name shows that name.

Asking with a mention

  • Type @ in any message box. Your assistant is first in the list.
  • A message that starts with @Ayati is never posted to the chat. Ayati writes the reply into the box, and you send it.
  • Add words to say what you want ("say yes, but ask for the agenda"), or none and let Ayati decide.
  • On a message, Reply with @Ayati in its toolbar does the same for that one message.
  • While a reply waits in the box, "@Ayati shorter" rewrites it.
  • Nobody else can mention your assistant. In their list, people see only their own.

The three positions

Try it

Press the button to have the other side write. Change the position with the control in the chat's header. Type @ in the message box, or press the mark beside a message, to ask for one reply. Open HR Desk in the list to see the same thing with an agent.

What stays, what goes, what is new

Stays

  • Replies are your own messages. Only you see the @Ayati tag; admins have the Audit log.
  • One per chat, never with another assistant, and a stop after 30 sent messages.
  • The mark on the chat's row in the list, beside Attach, and on a message's toolbar.
  • You can always write in the chat yourself.

Goes

  • The "take over from here" window. The mark on a message now asks for one reply to it.
  • The mark and name under a message, beside the time.
  • Stop, Resume and Delete, and the stopped and deleted states.
  • A first reply that goes out before you have seen anything Ayati wrote.
  • Titles. A chat has a position, not a named matter.

New

  • Ask me first: every reply waits in your message box.
  • @Ayati in any message box, and "Reply with @Ayati" on a message.
  • The @Ayati tag above what it wrote, saying whether it or you sent it.
  • The step-down rule, in place of "stopped".
  • Ayati's questions shown in the same chat and in Needs you.
  • A flag on a reply that shares something new or decides for you.
  • Later: starting positions for agents and for people.

Agents and people are different risks

Messages from agents

Mostly follow-up questions with a factual answer: which dates, which account, who covers. A wrong answer is cheap to correct. "Reply for me" is reasonable here, and "Ask me first" is a safe default for all of them.

Messages from people

A wrong tone or a wrong promise is hard to take back, and the other person believes you wrote it. Start with "Ask me first". Move one chat to "Reply for me" for one matter, after you have seen that the drafts are good.

Decisions for you

  1. Who sees the @Ayati tag? In this design only you do, as today. The alternative is to show "via @Ayati" to the other side on messages Ayati sent without you. That is more open, and it reverses the earlier decision that every message reads as yours.
  2. After a matter is settled: step down to Ask me first (my choice) or to Off?
  3. The mark on a message's toolbar changes from "take over from here" to "Reply with @Ayati": one reply to that message, which you send. Replying on its own is only ever turned on by the header switch.
  4. Cost. Ask me first runs the model once for each incoming message in the chats where it is on, against your assistant's budget.
